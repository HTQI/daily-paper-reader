Abstract 

We address question answering over large heterogeneous document collections: thou-sands of PDFs with tables and figures (open-domain M3DocVQA), chart-dense long reports (MMLongBench-Doc) and multi-document news corpora (MultiHop-RAG). A system must first find the right passages and then read them; current retrieval-augmented agents give one model both kinds of tools and let it schedule itself. We present KnowForge, a pipeline sep-arated by job : knowledge construction into a hybrid-retrieval knowledge-point index; a 

Locator agent that can only search; a code-scheduled bridge judge that forces the miss-ing second hop; a typed, size-bounded evi-dence pool that merges every search’s rank-ing; a Deep Reader agent that can only read text, images and tables; and a synthesizer with a format checker. With a self-hosted Qwen3.6-35B-A3B (3B active), KnowForge reaches F1 47.5 on the full M3DocVQA (2,441 questions, 3,368 PDFs): +11.0 over the pub-lished M3DocRAG system (ColPali + Qwen2-VL-7B, 36.5) and +7.1 over one-shot retrieve-and-answer on the same index and model; 53.5 with DeepSeek-V4-Flash. On the multi-document MultiHop-RAG it reaches 73.5 accu-racy, above the strongest published RAG and GraphRAG results (71.2 with Llama-3.1-70B) and the original GPT-4 baseline (56.0). On the single-document MMLongBench-Doc the pipeline lifts the same 3B-active model by 12.2 points, from 34.8 in one shot to 47.0, to the level of GPT-4o reading page images (46.3); only frontier vision-language models and RL-trained readers score higher. Per-job ablations on the full benchmarks attribute the gain: the Deep Reader is worth 3.8, 9.8 and 0 points on the three corpora, tracking how visual each is; merging both agents into one with all tools and twice the rounds loses 1.9 F1 because it keeps searching instead of reading; a round-budget sweep shows a step at four Locator rounds and a plateau beyond, and query latency scales with model calls rather than corpus size. To make these numbers checkable, we serve the three indexed benchmarks as read-only knowledge bases (free registration) and release all prompts and the runner code, so that retrieval recall can be reproduced without any model and answer accuracy with one’s own: https://github. com/metask-ai/knowforge-bench .

1 Introduction 

Answering a question over thousands of documents requires two different skills that pull in opposite directions. Finding the right passages is an open-ended search problem: there is always another query to try. Extracting the answer from a found passage is a closed reading problem: it requires opening a table, looking at a figure, or reading the paragraph around a hit, and it stops when the answer is in hand. Agentic RAG systems give a single model both kinds of tools and a round budget and let the model schedule itself (Li et al., 2025a,b, 2026). We find that when the two skills share one loop, the model systematically prefers the one with the immediate reward: it searches again rather than reading deeper. In our controlled comparison, a single agent with every tool and eight rounds made 34% more searches, read half as often, never opened a reading tool on 74% of questions, and scored 1.9 F1 lower than two agents with disjoint tools and four rounds each (§5.2). Prior systems take one of three routes. Single-pass retrieve-then-read systems such as M3DocRAG (Cho et al., 2024) and HiKEY (Shin et al., 2026) retrieve once and answer; they cannot recover the second document of a multi-hop ques-tion or open a figure they did not retrieve. Single-agent iterative systems (Search-o1 (Li et al., 2025a), WebThinker (Li et al., 2025b), DeepRead (Li et al., 2026), SimpleDoc (Jain et al., 2025)) let one model holding every tool decide when to search and when to read; we show below that such a model prefers to arXiv:2610.13128v1 [cs.AI] 9 Oct 2026 search. Multi-agent systems split roles by modality (MDocAgent (Han et al., 2025b)) or, most recently, by explore/refine/reflect stages on a single long doc-ument (MARDoc (Chen et al., 2026), MAGE-RAG (Zuo et al., 2026)), but keep overlapping tool sets, leave the second hop to the model, and report no full-benchmark measurement of what each role is worth (Table 1). Our step is to separate by job with physically disjoint tools, force the second hop in code, hand over through a typed evidence pool, and quantify each part against the published systems on three full public benchmarks. This paper argues that the unit of design for a document-QA agent should be the job , not the round. We describe KnowForge, a system whose pipeline is a chain of six jobs, each with its own tools and its own stopping rule (Figure 1): 1. Knowledge construction and hybrid retrieval 

(offline). Documents become Markdown (OCR where needed), split into text, image, table and example fragments; images and tables get VLM descriptions; atomic knowledge points are ex-tracted and linked by typed relations; everything is indexed for BM25 and dense retrieval with bounded graph expansion. 2. Locator. An agent whose only tools search and enumerate (search, list books, table of contents, table lookup). It cannot read. 3. Bridge judge. A code-level step that asks, af-ter locating, whether the evidence covers every hop of the question and, if not, issues one tar-geted follow-up search for the missing “bridge” document. 4. Multi-list ranked merge. All searches are merged into one 30K-token evidence pool that is typed : text, images, tables and examples are ranked in separate sub-pools with their own caps, because text hits score higher than me-dia hits and a single ranked list evicts figures and tables first. 5. Deep Reader. An agent whose only tools read (fragment context, read image, read table, media context). It has no search tool, so it cannot escape back into searching. 6. Answer. A synthesizer produces the answer from the pool and the reader’s notes; a format checker normalises it to the benchmark’s answer convention. Our contributions are: • A design principle and its realisation. The unit of design for a document-QA agent is the job, not the round: Locator and Deep Reader are separate agents with physically disjoint tool sets, the second hop is forced by a code-level bridge judge rather than left to the model, and the hand-off between them is a typed, size-bounded evidence pool that merges every search’s ranking so that figures and tables are not ranked out by text (§3). • Results against the published systems on three public benchmarks with a self-hosted 35B mixture-of-experts model (3B active): full open-domain M3DocVQA F1 47.5 vs. 36.5 for M3DocRAG (Cho et al., 2024) (+11.0) and 40.4 for one-shot retrieve-and-answer on the same index and model (+7.1); MultiHop-RAG accuracy 73.5 vs. 71.2 for the strongest pub-lished GraphRAG result (Llama-3.1-70B, Han et al., 2025a) and 56.0 for the original GPT-4 baseline (Tang and Yang, 2024); on the single-document MMLongBench-Doc, +12.2 points on the same model (34.8 →47.0), bringing a 3B-active model level with GPT-4o reading page images (46.3, Ma et al., 2024b; §5.1). The same design on DeepSeek-V4-Flash reaches 53.5 on M3DocVQA (§5.1, §5.6, §5.7). • Per-job attribution of the gain , all on the full benchmarks: the Deep Reader is worth 3.8 F1 on M3DocVQA, 9.8 points on MMLongBench-Doc and 0 on text-only MultiHop-RAG, tracking how visual the corpus is; role separation itself is worth 1.9 F1 against a single agent with ev-ery tool and twice the rounds; hybrid retrieval is worth 2.5 F1 inside the pipeline while typed graph expansion is neutral; a 12-configuration round-budget sweep shows a plateau below four Locator rounds, a step at four and nothing beyond (§5.2–§5.5). • Cost and open verification. Query latency is proportional to model calls and almost indepen-dent of corpus size (§5.8); the three indexed benchmarks are served as read-only knowledge bases (free account) with all prompts and runner code, so retrieval can be re-measured with no model and answers re-run with any model (§8).

3.1 Knowledge construction and hybrid retrieval 

Input files are converted to Markdown (marker for PDF with OCR fallback for scanned pages; a text extractor for HTML; native for Markdown). The Markdown is split into fragments typed as text, im-age, table, or example. Each image and table frag-ment receives a description from a vision-language model so that it participates in text retrieval; the original asset is kept for reading. From text frag-ments an LLM extracts atomic knowledge points (title, one-sentence summary, source fragment). Re-lations between knowledge points are typed (same-topic, extends, contrasts, supports, prerequisite) by recalling similar points with embeddings and let-ting an LLM assign the type only to recalled pairs. For M3DocVQA this yields 332K nodes and 299K edges (133K typed) over roughly 200K fragments. Retrieval is hybrid: BM25 over an inverted index and dense retrieval over an HNSW index are fused, and the top-8 anchors are expanded by one hop along typed relations; each hit carries its adjacent-fragment context and any figures or tables adjacent to it. One retrieval round makes no LLM call. 

3.2 Locator 

The Locator receives the question, the corpus overview, and a tool set that can only find :

search , list_books , book_toc , list_tables ,

query_table , get_table . It runs up to L rounds and stops when it declares the evidence located. It never sees a reading tool. 

Why a separate agent rather than a phase. An earlier version kept one agent and asked it, in the prompt, to spend its first rounds locating and the rest reading. The instruction had almost no ef-fect: on M3DocVQA the agent spent its budget on searching and read figures on a small minority of image questions, and the single-agent baseline P0 in §5.2 reproduces the pattern with the current prompts (74% of questions never see a reading tool). Searching is open-ended and rewarded im-mediately by new material; reading is bounded and costs a round without new hits. A model given both will search. The Locator therefore does not have the choice: its tool set contains nothing that reads. 

3.3 Bridge judge 

After the Locator stops, a fixed prompt asks the model whether the fragments found cover every en-tity the question chains through; if a hop is missing it names the missing entity and one follow-up query is issued for it. This is code-scheduled, bounded to one or two extra searches, and fires on roughly half of M3DocVQA questions. It exists because 78% of the documents missed by the earlier version of the pipeline were second-hop “bridge” documents. 

Why code and not the model. The Locator could in principle ask itself the same question. In practice it stopped as soon as it had material about the first entity: the name of the second document only becomes known after reading the first, and the agent that has just declared “located” does not reopen the search. Making the check a fixed step after the Locator’s stop costs one short model call (about 300 output tokens) and removes the decision from the loop. On the full M3DocVQA the judge fires on 48% of questions and issues 1.1 follow-up queries on average; on MultiHop-RAG, where ev-ery non-null question needs two to four articles, it is the single largest source of the 14.5-point rise in “all evidence found”. A variant that let the judge also drop evidence it thought irrelevant lost 3.1 F1 on the 300-question subset and was abandoned: a judge that only adds cannot hurt recall; one that also removes can. 

3.4 Multi-list ranked merge 

Every search made by the Locator and the bridge judge returns a ranked list. The lists are merged into one evidence pool that is handed, unchanged, to the Deep Reader and the synthesizer (the bridge judge sees its first 8K characters). Two design decisions matter. 

Cap. The earlier pipeline (M4) handed the reader only the first 12K tokens, which two searches al-ready exceed: of the 1,063 fragments the M4 reader opened, 865 came from the first search, and only 24 of 1,710 multi-search questions ever read a later-search fragment. Replaying the M4 queries offline, a 12K cap keeps the answer-bearing fragment visi-ble in 89.4% of questions, 20K in 95.8%, 30K in 99.0%; we set 30K. 

Typing. When the pool is full, blocks must be evicted, and text fragments score systematically higher than image, table and example fragments (a caption matches a question less literally than a paragraph), so a single ranked list evicts media first. In the M4 run the answer-bearing fragment sat at rank 1 in 58.5% of questions and within the top 8 in 91.7%; the remaining 8.3% sat at rank 9 or lower and were mostly figures, tables and examples (28% of answer-bearing fragments were tables, 12% images), exactly what table and figure questions need. The pool is therefore split into four sub-pools ranked separately (images 3K, tables 3K, examples 2K, text the remainder); within a sub-pool the top-3 hits of each search are protected and the lowest-scoring unprotected block is evicted first; expansion blocks and media attached to a text hit travel with it. In simulation the typed pool keeps the answer visible for 93.7% of questions against 86% for “top-5 per search”. 

3.5 Deep Reader 

The Deep Reader receives the evidence pool (§5.3) and a tool set that can only read :

fragment_context , read_media (one image or table per call, answered by a VLM), 

media_context , find_figure , list_media . It runs up to R rounds. It has no search tool; an earlier version that kept search available saw the reader defect back to searching on 66 of 76 calls. 

What the reader receives. The reader does not see the Locator’s conversation. It receives the question and the typed evidence pool (§5.3) ren-dered as numbered material blocks, each search hit with its adjacent context and any figures or tables attached to it as “[fig N ]” handles with page labels and fragment ids. Its tools take those ids: fragment_context expands a hit to its neigh-bours or returns the full VLM description of a figure whose caption was truncated in the pool; 

read_media sends one asset at a time with a single explicit question to the vision model and returns the answer verbatim (the prompt asks for the lo-cation of the evidence in the image and a one-line answer, and forbids using knowledge outside the image); media_context returns the caption and body text on the page of a figure, because for an image fragment the “adjacent” fragments are other images, not its caption. Every result is appended to the same pool, so the synthesizer later sees both what was found and what was read. 

3.6 Answer 

A synthesizer writes the answer from the pool and the reader’s notes. A format checker then rewrites it into the benchmark’s convention (shortest ex-act span; “Not answerable” or “Insufficient infor-mation” where the benchmark has such a class; Yes/No). Benchmarks differ only in this last layer and in one rule paragraph in the agent prompts; the tools are identical. 

4 Experimental Setup 

Benchmarks. M3DocVQA (Cho et al., 2024): 2,441 questions, 3,368 PDFs, one shared index; EM and token F1; 980 multi-hop questions; ev-idence modality text/table/image. A stratified 300-question subset ( mid300 ) is used for sweeps. 

MMLongBench-Doc (Ma et al., 2024b): 1,082 questions over 135 long PDFs (one index per doc-ument, as the task is closed-domain), 223 of them unanswerable; scored with the benchmark’s model-judge protocol (we report the judge accuracy and, in parentheses, strict accuracy where the judge score equals 1). MultiHop-RAG (Tang and Yang, 2024): 2,556 questions over 609 news articles, four types (inference, comparison, temporal, null); the official has_intersection accuracy. 

Models. Unless stated otherwise every text step runs on a self-hosted Qwen3.6-35B-A3B (FP8, 4×RTX 4090, sglang); image reading uses the same model’s vision input. DeepSeek-V4-Flash (API) is used for the model-axis experiment; Qwen2-VL-7B for the same-model reproduction of the M3DocRAG baseline. 

Systems. S4 : one-shot search-and-answer on our index (one hybrid search, answer). M5 : the full pipeline with L=4 , R=4 . P0 : the single-agent baseline (ReAct-style): one agent holding every tool of both roles, 8 rounds, with the same judge, pool, synthesizer and checker as M5, so that the only difference is that one model schedules search-ing and reading itself. P1 : the iterative-retrieval baseline : Locator, judge, pool and synthesizer but no Deep Reader, i.e. multi-round search that never opens a figure, table or context window. L+R vari-ants change only the round caps. Index variants replace hybrid retrieval by dense-only ( VEC ) or switch off graph expansion ( S3 ).

5 Results 

5.1 Main result and comparison with published systems 

Figure 2 places our results against the published systems; our claim is on the two multi-document benchmarks. On the full M3DocVQA the pipeline reaches EM 39.5 / F1 47.5 against 31.4 / 36.5 for M3DocRAG (44.6 / 53.5 with DeepSeek-V4-Flash); every later full-set system with a 7B reader reports EM ≤ 27.5 (Shin et al., 2026), while our index with a 7B reader in one shot gives 31.7 / 38.0. On MultiHop-RAG, 73.5 is above the strongest published RAG and GraphRAG results (70.3 and 71.2). On the single-document MMLongBench-Doc the pipeline lifts the same 35B-A3B model by 12.2 points, from 34.8 in one shot to 47.0 (§5.7), which puts a 3B-active open model level with GPT-4o reading page images (46.3); the systems above it are RL-trained readers or frontier vision-language models, whose advantage is in reading charts na-tively rather than in retrieval, which a single 50-page document does not require. Three factors separate on M3DocVQA (Table 6). With index and one-shot procedure fixed, the 35B model gains 2.4 F1 over the 7B reader (238:181 flips). With the model fixed, the pipeline gains 7.1 F1 over one-shot (95% CI [5 .5, 8.8] ; 447:255 at a 0.05 margin, sign test p < 10 −12 ), raises the share of questions with all supporting documents found from 73.3% to 84.6% and with the answer span visible from 66.4% to 77.2%. Since our index with the 7B reader already exceeds the published visual-retrieval system, the gain over M3DocRAG is not a stronger reader.

5.3 The evidence pool: cap and typing 

M5 differs from M4 (Table 6) in the bridge judge and the pool: +1.9 F1 on the full set (206:155; CI 

[0 .5, 3.3] , p = 0 .003 ), +3.6 on documents found, with table +2.3, image +1.7 and multi-hop +2.2. The cap itself is neutral: the 811 questions whose materials exceed 30K gain as much as the 1,630 that do not (28.9 →30.6 vs 53.9 →56.0); the pool’s value is that the reader sees everything up to 30K instead of the first 12K. Typing is what makes the larger pool safe: on the 300-question subset a single ranked list with cosine eviction (pool v1) scored 23.3 F1 on the questions that hit the cap, against 30.6 for the typed pool, because tables were evicted below text of the same search.

Keeping M5 fixed and changing only the in-dex: dense-only retrieval drops F1 to 45.0 ( −2.5,155:226; CI [−4.0, −1.1] , p = 0 .007 ) with first-search R@8 78.8 →72.5; switching off typed graph expansion gives 47.0 ( −0.5, 180:201; p = 0 .56 ), first-search recall slightly up and answer visibility slightly down. 5.6 Model axis 

On the same 300 questions, one-shot →M5 gains 7.8 F1 with Qwen3.6 (43.1 →50.9) and 6.1–6.9 with DeepSeek-V4-Flash (47.2 →54.1 / 53.3). The document-found rate is identical for both models in one shot (71.0) and rises to 82–84 for both in the pipeline: the retrieval layer is model-independent, and the pipeline’s gain is a shift of about the same size on both. On the full set Flash reaches 53.5 (Fig-ure 2), 6.0 above Qwen3.6 at the same pipeline, so the design does not need a weak model to show its effect. An earlier version of the pipeline run in full with DeepSeek-V4-Pro reached F1 51.1 against 50.8 for Flash on the same version, so the two API models sit in one band and we kept the cheaper one. At the small end, Qwen2-VL-7B driven through the same multi-round protocol did worse than its own one-shot (mid300 37.4 vs 40.6) and Qwen3-VL-8B only tied it (41.5–42.3 vs 41.3): the pipeline found them 9 more points of documents, but they could not convert the extra evidence. The protocol assumes a model that can follow a tool-call for-mat across rounds; below that threshold the gain is retrieval-only.

(80 →3,368 PDFs; 3.7K →200K fragments) raises one retrieval round by 7% (0.113 →0.121 s). The cost of scale is accuracy, not time: on the same 54 questions one-shot R@8 falls 99.4 →78.1 and F1 38.1 →31.9 as distractors grow, which is why the second-hop search exists.

6 Analysis 

Where the remaining errors are. Of the M3DocVQA questions whose supporting docu-ment is the first hit yet F1 < 0.5, a 100-question sample classified by DeepSeek-V4-Flash and inde-pendently re-checked by a second LLM reviewer with the full traces (88% agreement; Appendix G) attributes 31 to misreading or not opening an im-age, 23 to answer granularity the token-F1 metric penalises (“rifle” vs “gun”), 19 to the wrong field in the right document, 13 to refusing with the an-swer present in the text, 9 to the wrong table row or column and 5 to multi-hop assembly. With retrieval at 92% recall, more than half of the residual loss is not retrieval. 

A worked example. Appendix F traces one two-document question through all four configurations. 

“Which driver with a time/retired of +1 lap in the Trenton Twin Indy of 1979 has won the Indy 500 the most times?” (gold: Rick Mears). The race page’s prose names the winners; the “+1 lap” finishers ex-ist only in the results table, stored as an image. One-shot answers from the prose (“Al Unser”, wrong, 2 s). The iterative-retrieval baseline P1 searches the race page four times, and its bridge judge, reason-ing from the prose, asks for the Indy 500 wins of the wrong three drivers; the follow-up search does retrieve the List of Indianapolis 500 winners , but with nobody reading the table the answer is again “Al Unser”. The full pipeline runs the same Locator and judge, then the Deep Reader opens the results table with read_media and reads the four +1-lap finishers (Dallenbach, Mears, Sneva, Ongais); with the winners list already in the pool the synthesizer answers “Rick Mears” (46 s under the 4-way har-ness). The single-agent P0 also solves this question, by reading the table right after its first search; the point of Table 2 is that on average it does so far less often. 

False refusals. On the two benchmarks with an unanswerable class the pipeline still refuses too often on answerable questions (MultiHop-RAG 9.8%, MMLongBench-Doc 274 of 859), although the multi-round configuration halves the rate rela-tive to one-shot (18.1% →9.8%; 475 →274). The residual is the price of the conservative answer con-vention; relaxing it trades the unanswerable bucket one for one. 

Honest boundaries. (i) Cross-page questions on MMLongBench-Doc stay low under every configu-ration (10–22% of 358); merging evidence across pages is a shared weakness. (ii) Graph expansion is neutral here. (iii) The round-cap plateau was measured on one model whose loop shows the re-maining budget; the reader stops early when little remains rather than being cut off. (iv) Closed-book scores on M3DocVQA are non-trivial for strong models (Flash 43.0, Qwen3.6 32.2) because the corpus is Wikipedia; we therefore report gains over the same model’s one-shot rather than over closed-book. 

7 Discussion 

Which model can drive this pipeline. The pro-tocol asks the model to emit one JSON object per round, to stop with a placeholder rather than an answer, and to keep a thought to one or two sentences. Qwen3.6-35B-A3B and DeepSeek-V4-Flash follow it; Qwen2-VL-7B and Qwen3-VL-8B do not reliably: on the 300-question subset 113 of 300 runs of the 7B ended in a forced stop of both agents, and their multi-round scores (37.4 and 41.5–42.3) did not beat their own one-shot (40.6 and 41.3) even though the pipeline found them 9 more points of documents. The extra evidence was there and could not be converted. Prompt choice is also model-dependent: the official M3DocRAG answer template was the best of three prompts for the 7B model and the worst for the 35B model (−3.3 F1 on the subset, −1.4 on the full set in-side the pipeline), and a “positive phrasing” rewrite that removed every prohibition from the prompts lost 4.6 on the subset. We therefore did not tune prompts per model, and report the protocol thresh-old as a limitation: below it, the gain of the design is retrieval-only. 

Round budgets are upper bounds. The sweep in §5.4 is often read as “more rounds help until four”. The usage data (Appendix C) says some-thing narrower: from a cap of 3 upward the Locator reaches its cap on only 44–57% of questions, the Reader makes no call on 55–76%, and the amount of reading depends only on the Reader’s own cap (0.24, 0.38, 0.48, 0.67–0.73, 0.85, 0.90 calls per question for caps 1–6), not on the Locator’s. Nei-ther cap alone explains the step from 3+3 to 4+4 (+4.7 F1): with the other cap at 4, the Locator’s fourth round is worth 2.3 and the Reader’s 0.6, al-though the Reader’s fourth round raises reads by half (0.47 →0.67–0.73) while its cap-hit rate moves only from 23% to 17–18%. Raising both caps fur-ther raised reads again and F1 not at all. The model appears to stop early when it can see that little bud-get remains, and to read more when it has room, up to a point. This is an observation about one model in a loop that displays the remaining budget, not a general law; the threshold will move with the model and the loop design. 

What the graph layer is for. The knowledge-point graph (typed relations between atomic state-ments) was built for browsing and summarisation in the product, and we tested whether it also helps retrieval. Inside the pipeline, expanding the top hits along typed edges is neutral (+0.5 F1, p = 0 .56 ): it slightly raises answer visibility on single-hop questions and slightly lowers first-search recall on the rest by displacing BM25 hits from a fixed-size list. Hybrid lexical-plus-dense retrieval, by con-trast, is worth 2.5 F1 inside the pipeline while only 0.5 in one shot: multi-round search converts better ranking into found documents, because the Locator decides what to read from the first few hits. We report the neutral result rather than dropping the component, since its cost is 45 ms per round and its value may differ on corpora with denser cross-document relations than a sample of Wikipedia. 

Deployment. The same six-job chain is the question-answering path of the product the corpora were indexed with; the benchmark harness differs from the product only in the answer-format checker and one rule paragraph per benchmark. The design decision that carried over most directly is the dis-joint tool set: in production the reader agent has no search tool for the same reason as in the benchmark. The 27-second latency is acceptable for a research assistant and not for a chat interface; the first vis-ible event at 1.2 s and the streamed synthesis are what make it usable. 

Outlook. The pipeline’s gain is largest where the model is weakest at scheduling itself, and the jobs are simple enough that each could be served by a smaller or specialised model: a Locator needs query reformulation and stopping, a Reader needs tool discipline and vision, a judge needs one structured decision. Whether a set of small, job-specialised agents can match a large general model on this task is the natural next experiment; the per-stage model overrides in the released runner are there to make it easy to run. 

8 Open Verification 

The three benchmark indexes are served read-only by the product’s retrieval API to anyone with a free account (an API key plus the index identi-fiers). We release the identifiers, fetch scripts for the official question sets, the scoring scripts, all prompts, and a reference runner that takes one configuration file (model endpoint, API address, index identifier) and produces a scored run. Athird party can recompute document recall from the search endpoint alone, run the reference agent with their own model, or score their own agent against the same indexes. The served indexes were rebuilt from the same converted documents and ex-tracted knowledge points as our runs, with figure descriptions and typed relations regenerated by the same model family; on the 299-question MultiHop-RAG subset with the same Qwen3.6 endpoint, the released runner scores 73.9 against 71.9 for our in-process harness (16:10 flips), inside the run-to-run band. Our model endpoint is not exposed and we redistribute neither documents nor raw out-puts; images are read by downloading the asset into the third party’s own model. 9 Conclusion 

Splitting a document-QA agent by job, with dis-joint tools and code-scheduled hand-offs, beats one-shot retrieval by 7 F1 on the full M3DocVQA and the published systems on both multi-document benchmarks, and makes each job’s value measur-able: the reader matters where evidence is visual, the locator and forced second hop everywhere, ex-tra rounds and the graph layer nowhere. 

Limitations 

The main line runs on one self-hosted model, but the pipeline is validated in full on two model fami-lies: Qwen3.6-35B-A3B and DeepSeek-V4-Flash (F1 47.5 and 53.5 on M3DocVQA; 47.0 and 49.0 on MMLongBench-Doc), with DeepSeek-V4-Pro run in full on the earlier pipeline version (51.1, in the same band as Flash). Smaller open mod-els (Qwen2-VL-7B, Qwen3-VL-8B) were run on the 300-question subset only, where the multi-round protocol did not beat their own one-shot; the claims therefore hold for models that can fol-low a multi-round tool protocol, and the round-cap plateau in particular was measured only on Qwen3.6. M3DocVQA is Wikipedia-derived and partially memorised by large models, which is why we anchor on one-shot rather than closed-book. MMLongBench-Doc is scored by a model judge (we use our own Qwen3.6 as judge, following the benchmark’s protocol) and judge choice moves scores by 2–3 points. Latency figures are for one hardware configuration and one serial client. We did not tune prompts per benchmark beyond one rule paragraph and the format checker, and we re-port a prompt variant that helped on a smoke test and hurt on the full set as a negative result in the appendix.

Shared loop protocol (both agents). The model answers each round with a JSON object 

{"thought": ..., "tool": ..., "args": ...} or stops with {"thought": ..., "final": "ok"} . The rules state that the thought is one or two sentences, that the agent must never write the answer text in the loop (it is generated by the syn-thesizer from the collected materials and any an-swer written here is discarded), and that the agent should stop as soon as the materials suffice. Every tool result is followed by a one-line reminder of the same stopping rule; we found in-context reminders far more effective than rules at the top of the system prompt for the models used.

Bridge judge (verbatim, English).                                                                                   

> You audit whether collected materials are sufficient to answer a question that may need TWO hops. A “bridge entity” is a person/place/work/organization that the materials reveal as the answer to the FIRST part of the question, while the question then asks something ABOUT that entity (its county, founder, release year, genre, ...). That entity’s own document must be retrieved to answer. Decide: If the materials already contain the final answer, or the question is single-hop, output need_more=false. If abridge entity is identified but its own document has NOT been retrieved yet (the materials only mention it in passing), output need_more=true with 1-2 short search queries that retrieve that entity’s document plus the asked attribute (e.g. “Bay Lake Florida county”). Also output need_more=true if the question names asecond document/title that none of the searched queries targeted. Never repeat a query already searched. Output JSON only: {"need_more": true|false, "bridge_entities": [...], "queries": [...], "reason": "<one sentence>"}

You are in the deep-reading stage : the locat-ing stage has found the relevant materials (see the material summary in the task); your job is to get every detail. Content transcribed verba-tim in the materials (table rows, text copied from figures) may be trusted directly; stop when it suf-fices. Visual attributes (counts, colours, posi-tions, expressions, chart values whose captions were cropped) must be looked at : pass the fig-ure’s fragment_id (one or several) and one ex-plicit question to read_media and trust what it sees; caption text is only a summary and is not evidence. Use fragment_context for the text around a hit and query_table for exact table val-ues. You have no search tool : the materials are those given in the task, and your whole job is to read them deeply; read before you conclude, and never assert “not present” from a caption. The descriptions under “related figures/tables” are truncated and the answer may be in the cut part; read the full description with fragment_context

Single-agent rules for P0. P0 uses the two-stage narrative of an earlier single-agent version (“your tool rounds are a scarce budget; spend the first 2–3 on locating, the rest on deep reading; when results largely repeat what you have, locating is done and you must switch to reading rather than re-searching”), plus the enumeration, figure-number, two-sided and truncated-caption rules above. Its tool set is the union of both roles’ tools and its round cap is 8. Judge, pool, synthesizer and checker are identical to M5.

Negative results on prompts. Two alternatives were tested on the 35B model and rejected: the official M3DocRAG text_rag template in place of our synthesizer ( −3.3 F1 on mid300, −1.4 on the full set inside the multi-round pipeline), and a “positive phrasing” rewrite that removed every prohibition ( −4.6 on mid300). On the 7B model the official template was the best of the three, so prompt choice is model-dependent and we did not reuse the small model’s prompt on the large one. Two MultiHop-RAG variants that forced a Yes/No conclusion when two articles agree were −12 .9 on a 31-question smoke set and were reverted before the full run. 

B Full M3DocVQA tables 

Pool cap. Of the 2,441 questions, 811 exceeded the 30K-token pool and were trimmed; M5 gains over M4 on both groups (untrimmed 53.9 →56.0, trimmed 28.9 →30.6), so the cap itself is neutral and the value of the pool is that the reader sees everything up to 30K rather than the first 12K. 

Noise. Three full runs of the 4+4 configuration on mid300 give 51.1 / 50.7 / 50.9; two full-set runs of the earlier pipeline differ by 0.3 F1 with sym-metric flips (179:184). The per-question paired F1 standard deviation is 0.344, giving a paired stan-dard error of ±2.0 on 300 questions and ±0.7 on the full set. 

C Round-cap sweep: usage 

Cutting one role at a time (single-role lines in Ta-ble 3): Locator 4 →3 costs 2.3 F1 and 4 →1 costs 2.1; Reader 4 →3 costs 0.6 and 4 →1 costs 3.3; cut-ting both to 3 costs 4.7. The costs are not additive: the two single cuts at 3 sum to 2.9, while cutting both together costs 4.7, so each role’s fourth round matters more when the other role is also short of rounds. 

D Index construction cost 

The resulting M3DocVQA graph has 332K knowledge-point nodes and 299K edges, 133K of them typed. Tokens per Markdown character are 2.4 (M3DocVQA), 1.7 (MMLongBench-Doc) and 0.6 (MultiHop-RAG): PDF corpora with figures cost three to four times more to index than clean text, and pre-processing alone is 30–41% of the PDF cost.

Refusals. On MultiHop-RAG the format checker maps every refusal to ‘Insufficient information’. The pipeline predicted it on 705 / 612 / 515 / 532 questions (S4 / 1+1 / M5 / P1), of which 409 / 318 / 220 / 240 were on answerable questions.
