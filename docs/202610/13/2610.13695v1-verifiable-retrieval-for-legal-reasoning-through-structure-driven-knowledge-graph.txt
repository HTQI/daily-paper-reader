# Verifiable Retrieval for Legal Reasoning through Structure-driven Knowledge Graph 

Ibrahim Siddig 1,*, Sviatoslav Tugeev 1 and Munir Georges 1

> 1

Technische Hochschule Ingolstadt, Esplanade 10, 85049 Ingolstadt, Germany 

Abstract 

Any automation within reliability-critical contexts such as medicine or law has to be transparent and verifiable. Despite their advantages, current LLM-based systems do not meet these requirements due to their inherent black-box nature. Retrieval Augmented Generation (RAG) attempts to ground the generative process in the relevant interpretable documents, thereby reducing the risk of AI ’hallucinations’ and potentially enabling verification of the initial generation context. However, as the number and size of potential sources increases, naive retrieval approaches tend to perform worse, ignoring critical context elements. In this paper we aim to tackle this problem in the context of German Legal Code. Using structure-driven parsing to create a Knowledge Graph (KG) for the source document navigation and retrieval, we compare several KG-informed chunking and retrieval strategies and evaluate their effect on the accuracy of retrieved citations. 

Keywords 

Knowledge graph, Legal reasoning, Structure-driven parsing, Transparency in AI, AI in legal systems, Graph-RAG architecture 

# 1. Introduction 

The practical quality of legal answers is determined not only by their linguistic coherence and relevance to the context of the question, but by the degree to which they are supported by the relevant legal documents. This support is usually demonstrated by citing specific provisions, enabling the end user to verify legal validity of the answer. In practice, a legal text that is linguistically correct but cites the wrong sources or cites them incorrectly is not just useless, but harmful, as it looks trustworthy while being factually wrong or incomplete. This is especially relevant in the context of AI systems, which are already known for their ability to produce false but extremely plausible results [1]. In the context of RAG, where the output is generated based on the relevant law texts, this problem can be separated into three key issues: • Whether all source documents or their segments which contain information necessary for a valid legal judgment are retrieved based on the initial query (factual description of the case). • Whether the generated answer corresponds to the facts of the case and the content of the retrieved documents. • Whether the retrieved documents are cited correctly within the final answer. For German law, key prerequisites for studying this topic have been available for some time. The OpenLegalData corpus 1 [2, 3] provides 424 000 German court decisions with structured metadata, separated into the following sections: Tenor which explains the final decision, Tatbestand which shows statements of case facts; and Entscheidungsgründe which states the reasoning behind the final case decision. This extensive data is, however, insufficient, without a reliable way of processing it. This paper seeks to address the first issue in the RAG pipeline by exploring the viability of a structure-driven knowledge graph for addressing accuracy and token efficiency of retrieval in legal AI. In order to do that, we attempt to answer the following research questions:

# 2. Related Work 

[2] released 250 000 German court decisions with structured metadata; the subsequent segmentation work of [3] released a cleaned JSONL corpus of 251 038 decisions with Tenor / Tatbestand / Entschei-dungsgründe splits. The Bundesrecht library [4] covers the upstream task of parsing German statutory reference strings, providing a structured JSONL corpus of 6873 federal laws. Our knowledge graph is complementary rather than competing. While Bundesrecht optimizes for citation-string parsing and depth-aware lookup over a flat representation, we build a graph with defined cross-reference edges. [5] proposed a hierarchical and temporal graph-RAG architecture for legal norms but did not evaluate on real court decisions. [6] presented NyayaRAG, a statute-plus-case RAG framework for Indian legal judgment prediction; their evaluation conflates flat statute retrieval and case retrieval. [7] at RAGE-KG 2025 benchmarked KG-based RAG systems on legal documents. Our work differs in scoping the retrieval knowledge base to statute law alone, holding case law out entirely as evaluation gold, and measuring citation faithfulness at the level courts actually cite. Prior evaluations of legal citation accuracy typically match at a flat or abstract level [8, 9], which cannot distinguish § 574 Abs. 2 Nr. 2 from § 574 Abs. 3. Our structured metric closes this gap by accounting for strict hierarchical subdivisions critical for legal documents. Empirical evaluations demonstrate that language models fail to robustly utilize long inputs, exhibiting severe performance degradation when critical information is positioned in the middle of the context window [10]. Modern frameworks increasingly employ multi-level hierarchical segmentation, ensuring semantic continuity across diverse text scales while preserving structural layout [11]. The necessity of token-efficient graph retrieval is further highlighted by TERAG [12], which adapts Personalized PageRank [13] to cut GraphRAG token costs down to 3% –11% while maintaining retrieval fidelity. In a similar spirit of scaling graph-based retrieval to large legal corpora, our work demonstrates that optimizing node granularity directly tackles this token bottleneck, delivering high recall efficiency per token.

# 3. Methodology 

3.1. Knowledge graph construction 

To build our KG, a set of 58 codes was compiled based on the citations in the Entscheidungsgründe 

and law references sections of the OpenLegalData corpus. The full hierarchy, from the code down to the Buchstabe , is extracted from the official consolidated text published at gesetze-im-internet.de using our custom regex parser, established in [14] and expanded here with additional patterns to enable processing of documents with more diverse structures. Citations occurring within a statute are tagged during parsing and represented as directed cites_* edges; all 22 ,253 of them resolve within a single code, so the result is a forest of 58 disjoint code graphs. The constructed KG consists of the single root node not related to any specific document, which then diverges into multiple subtrees, each representing specific legal code. While there are no connecting edges between the subtrees, the decision to unify them into a single graph was guided by the fact that in the OpenLegalData corpus multiple codes are regularly cited together, demonstrating that legal decisions require access to an arbitrary number of codes simultaneously. Unlike the framework described in [5], the resulting KG includes only two types of edges: contains_* , to represent the hierarchical structure of the document, and cites_* ,to represent citation references. 3.2. Experiment setup 

All text segments, including statutory chunks and query fields, are mapped to a continuous vector space using KaLM-Embedding-Gemma3-12B-2511 [15]. This 12-billion parameter text embedding and retrieval model is optimized for high-density retrieval and achieves state-of-the-art multilingual performance on benchmarks such as MMTEB. To evaluate the impact of different chunking granularities and multi-hop graph retrieval strategies, we establish the pipeline illustrated in Fig. 2.

3.2.1. Chunking strategies 

Structure-driven KG enables granular chunking of the original document, selecting chunks based on different node levels. Different chunking sizes offer a tradeoff between the probability that any retrieved chunk will include relevant information and the increasing context window, coupled with a reduced effect of any single chunk on the final model output. To compare different chunking strategies for processing legal documents using KG, in this paper we explored the effectiveness of chunking on the following levels, listed in increasing level of granularity: § · Absatz · Satz · Nummer · Buchstabe 4

These levels were compared to each other and against a naive chunking approach, which did not utilize KG and simply chunked the texts of the legal codes into consecutive 100-word chunks, ignoring the document structure. The constant 100-w size was chosen to ensure structure-agnostic approach. 

3.2.2. One-hop retrieval strategies 

As each text chunk is linked to a specific node, a set of relevant text chunks can be further expanded using KG edges. As KG includes two types of edges, we evaluated three different one-hop expansion strategies: • Parent node. This strategy utilized only the contains_* edge type, representing hierarchical links in KG. As a result, this expansion included the texts of parent nodes of the initially retrieved top-k.• Citation. This strategy utilized only the cites_* edge type, representing reference links in KG, including the nodes cited directly in the initially retrieved top-k.• Hybrid. This strategy utilized both types of links at the same time. These strategies were compared to the baseline where only the originally retrieved top-k nodes were used.

# 4. Results & Discussion 

As shown in Table 1, structure-aware chunking strategy with higher granularity consistently dominates across all evaluation depths. Specifically, the Nummer chunking granularity achieves the highest ToER at the Code (14.23) and § (3.25) evaluation depths. Meaning that this approach retrieves the most relevant chunks of the source texts at the lowest token cost, when evaluated up to this depth. Chunking at Satz level yields the strongest performance at every deeper evaluation depth, reaching 2.28 at Absatz ,1.53 at Satz , and 2.79 at Nummer . Both configurations recover approximately three times more citations per 1,000 words than the Naive-100w chunking baseline, showing 14.23 vs. 4.19 at Code , 3.25 vs. 1.10 at 

§, and 2.79 vs. 0.81 at Nummer evaluation depths. Notably, this performance increase is driven by the chunking granularity itself rather than merely introducing structural awareness. Chunking at higher levels, such as § (Paragraph) , underperforms, even falling below the unstructured Naive-100w baseline with a ToER of only 3.06 at Code and 0.85 at § evaluation depths. Chunking at Absatz then represents an intermediate transition (7.78 and 1.93, respectively). Across all chunking granularities, ToER drops abruptly between the Code and § evaluation depth, such as Nummer falling from 14 .23 → 3.25 . This sharp drop exposes the heavy efficiency penalty the approach pays when moving from broad, code-level retrieval down to precise citations. This paper addressed the challenge of verifiable retrieval in legal AI by introducing a structure-driven Knowledge Graph (KG) approach for German statutory law. By evaluating various chunking and one-hop context expansion strategies, we demonstrated that aligning retrieval mechanisms with the inherent hierarchical structure of legal codes is essential for maximizing both citation recall and token efficiency. 

Limitations Even under the best configuration, the absolute ToER is modest and decays steeply with evaluation depth. The strongest chunking granularities reach a ToER of ≈ 14 .2 at the Code evaluation depth, but only ≈ 3.2 at the § and ≈ 1.5 at the Satz evaluation levels. This decay reflects the inherent difficulty of the task. This difficulty arises due to the domain gap between factual case descriptions (Tatbestand ) and abstract legal norms. Consequently, dense retrieval embeddings struggle to bridge non-legal narrative language to precise statutory formulations without explicit domain adaptation. 

Future Work The results of this paper can be expanded upon in multiple ways. First, a comparison between multiple embedders would provide a valuable insight into the correlation between embedding strategy and the effectiveness of the KG-driven chunking approach. Second, structure-driven KG could benefit from the addition of entity-driven KG, which would encode additional relevance links within the document. Finally, as different chunking levels utilize different proportions of context windows, additional experiments into comparative token efficiency with varied top-k retrieval between KG node levels are a valuable avenue of future research. Finally, while granular chunking improves retrieval efficiency, its direct effect on downstream generation quality and hallucination mitigation remains to be validated, given that this study explicitly isolates retrieval performance from downstream LLM artifacts. Finally, evaluation of retrieved items by legal specialists with relevant domain knowledge would provide valuable insight into their sufficiency for informed legal judgment by a human specialist.
